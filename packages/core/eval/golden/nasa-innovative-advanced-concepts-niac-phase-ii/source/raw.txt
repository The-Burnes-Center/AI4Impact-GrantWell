2. Executive Summary 
This Appendix, the SpaceTech REDDI 2026 NRA, and the Grants and Cooperative Agreement 
Manual (GCAM) contain all the information needed to respond to this call. The information 
presented in this Appendix supplements the information provided in the SpaceTech REDDI 
2026 NRA. Where this Appendix and the SpaceTech REDDI NRA are in conflict, this 
Appendix takes precedence. Where this Appendix and the GCAM are in conflict, this Appendix 
takes precedence. 
 
NIAC Phase II seeks proposals that will continue the exploration and development of 
revolutionary advanced concepts started through a NIAC Phase I award. While the Phase I 
study establishes basic feasibility and clear potential benefit, the Phase II study extends, refines, 
and builds upon this foundation. Phase II studies address key remaining unknowns, 
assumptions, risks, and paths forward. This should explicitly include cost, performance vs. 
alternatives, development steps and associated schedule, and key enabling technologies. These 
results aim to provide a sound basis for NASA to consider the concept for further development 
and a future mission, substantiated with a description of applicable scientific and technical 
disciplines necessary for development.

C. Program Description 
1. Overview 
This call invites continuation of previously funded NIAC Phase I concepts that are especially 
promising and could one day change the possible in aerospace. 
 
The NASA Innovative Advanced Concepts (NIAC) Program supports innovative research 
through multiple phases of study. Phase I awards are nine-month efforts funded at up to $175k, 
to explore the overall feasibility and viability of visionary concepts. Phase II awards are for up 
to two years at a total funding level of up to $750k per award, to further develop the most 
promising Phase I concepts, and to explore potential infusion options within and beyond 
NASA. Concepts should be sufficiently well developed at the end of Phase II to seek follow-on 
development funds from government or commercial programs external to NIAC. However, in 
rare instances there may be a compelling need for the strategic investment of additional NIAC 
funds to further advance Phase II technologies with clearly defined transition paths into other 
NASA, government, or commercial programs. Phase III awards are designed to meet this need, 
with the anticipated selection of up to one new award per year. Phase III studies typically are 
funded for a maximum duration of two years at a total funding level of up to $2M per award. 
Future Phase III opportunities may be solicited under separate NOFOs.  
 
2. Phase II Goals and Objectives 
Through NIAC’s contributions to STMD’s sustained, deliberate investment in a low- 
Technology Readiness Level (TRL), early-stage portfolio, NASA seeks exciting, unexplored, 
credible aerospace concepts with at least one mission application that addresses NASA goals or 
offers wider benefits for space or aeronautics applications. 
 
NIAC focuses on early studies of visionary aerospace concepts. These aerospace architectures, 
proposed in the context of a reference mission, will generally advance from TRL 1-4 in 
maturity and typically explore transformational concepts that could shape future aerospace 
architectures. Please refer to the link provided at the end of this Appendix for TRL definitions. 
 
In Phase I, NIAC specifies that the concept must be: 
• Relevant to NASA’s Vision and Mission 
o NASA’s Vision: Exploring the secrets of the universe for the benefit of all. 
o NASA’s Mission: NASA explores the unknown in air and space, innovates for 
the benefit of humanity, and inspires the world through discovery. 
• Innovative and Offer High Potential Impact 
o Enables an entirely new mission, greatly improves mission execution, or offers a 
significant leap in capabilities.  
o Breaks new ground, changing the conversation on future mission possibilities, or 
significantly contributing to scientific knowledge and understanding. 
o There are demonstrable benefits to conducting the study now, even if the 
ultimate vision is considered far-term or high-risk. 
o Generally, a NIAC concept is based on new technology or science, but it can 
also be based on existing technology or science applied to a mission in

significantly new, innovative, and impactful ways.  
• Credible and Reasonable 
o Technically sound; based on solid scientific/engineering principles. Plausibly 
implementable; at least one reasonable path for further development and 
eventual implementation must be described.  
• Proposed in the Context of a Representative Mission 
NIAC concepts must be incorporated into a well-defined representative mission 
to contextualize the innovation for development and analysis.  
o A representative mission involves the architecture, science objectives, 
operations, logistics, system-level design, etc. that frames the particular 
innovation being proposed. Innovative technologies, such as an instrument or 
detector, should not be submitted as a standalone development to NIAC. Rather, 
they should be incorporated within a representative mission.  
o A mission is a plan to achieve one or more clear objectives (e.g., advancing 
exploration, science, aerospace operations) to benefit NASA or the larger 
aerospace community. 
o The representative mission need not be a current or planned NASA mission. Any 
aerospace mission (previously considered or hypothetical) may be selected. 
o To highlight the innovation and its potential, proposers must provide a 
rudimentary comparison with alternative approaches within the proposal and 
outline a more extensive study in the proposed work plan. 
 
NIAC Phase II awards continue the exploration and development of revolutionary advanced 
concepts initiated through a NIAC Phase I award. While the Phase I study establishes basic 
feasibility and clear potential benefit, the Phase II study extends, refines, and builds upon this 
foundation. 
 
Phase II studies address key remaining unknowns, assumptions, risks, and paths forward. 
This should explicitly include a roadmap to implementation, development steps and associated 
schedule, and key enabling technologies. These results should provide a sound basis for NASA 
to consider the concept for further development and a future mission, substantiated with a 
description of applicable scientific and technical disciplines necessary for development. Toward 
that end, the proposed Phase II study must: 
• Continue to develop the concept studied in the Phase I award. Refinements or 
advancements identified in the Phase I effort are expected to be incorporated into Phase 
II, but the Phase II concept must be essentially based on the Phase I concept. 
• Continue to assess the concept in a representative mission. The main emphases should 
include establishing viability, developing key aspects of the concept, and comparing 
properties/performance with those of current or alternative mission approaches. 
Concepts that may support multiple missions should discuss the range but must feature a 
detailed analysis for a specific representative mission. 
• Assess the benefits and trades of the proposed concept. Analyze the relationships 
between the concept's potential benefits, cost, risks, and performance. 
Identify key enabling technologies and develop a technology roadmap and 
recommendations for further development.

The Phase II proposal shall consist of the electronic cover page that is automatically generated 
in NSPIRES, along with the proposal attachment (e.g., technical, budget justification, 
biographical sketches, etc.). The proposal attachment should not exceed 20MB.

text and captions may not, on average across a solid block of text, exceed 15 characters per 
horizontal inch, including spaces, though text within figures and tables may be smaller if still 
judged by the reviewers to be readable. Proposals may not have more than 5.5 lines per vertical 
inch of text, must have at least one-inch margins, be set for US letter size (8.5x11) paper, and 
may not have expository text necessary for the proposal located solely in figures, tables, or their 
captions. Failure to comply with formatting requirements may eliminate the proposal, or parts 
of the proposal, from review. 
 
Also, any offeror using TeX or LaTeX is required first to create a DVI file and then convert the 
DVI file to Postscript and then to PDF. See the NSPIRES PDF Guidelines for more information 
on creating PDF documents compliant with NSPIRES.

F. Application Review Information 
1. Application Evaluation Criteria 
The following criteria will be used to evaluate proposals and inform selections. These 
evaluation criteria supersede the criteria defined in SpaceTech REDDI 2026 section F.1. All 
criteria are of approximately equal weight. Offerors shall address each criteria below to make 
the most compelling case for the proposed concept.  
 
 NIAC Phase II Evaluation Criteria 
#1 Concept Progression  
a. The proposed research objectives build on the Phase I study findings while also 
demonstrating unexplored, exciting, and valuable aspects to investigate beyond the Phase I 
study. 
b. The analysis completed in Phase I is credible. However, unknowns remain that are not 
readily determined, thereby warranting further study. 
#2 Technical Study Approach 
a. A work plan is proposed that demonstrates an understanding of major issues and proposes a 
logical, strategic, and judicious course of study to address significant unknowns, barriers, 
and necessary technology development. 
b. The proposed study and its products are likely to significantly advance NASA’s 
understanding of concept feasibility and benefit. 
c. The work plan includes analysis of one specific mission demonstrating the potential for 
substantial benefits relative to the state of the art, or the enabling of an entirely new and 
highly appealing mission capability. 
 d. Team members are sufficiently well qualified, and adequate resources are identified to 
complete the study as proposed. 
e. The study timeline is commensurate with the scope of the proposed budget. There is 
sufficient budget for labor, materials and supplies, mandatory travel, etc. to complete the 
study.

f. A plan to develop a technology roadmap has been adequately described and there is a 
mechanism for identifying the enabling technologies, which culminates in a feasible 
implementation of the concept. 
g. The proposed study continues the science or technology developed from the NIAC Phase I 
Award based on the provided Key Findings. 
#3 Representative Mission 
a. The proposal describes a specific representative mission in sufficient detail to enable the 
assessment of proposed technology benefits by a cognizant reviewer, to include rough 
preliminary estimates of total system mass, power and volume. A list of missions that may 
benefit from the innovation is not sufficient.  
b. The underlying mission architecture is technically sound, with at least one plausible 
implementation path identified and discussed. 
#4 Potential Impact 
a. The concept is exciting and offers compelling potential benefit. If successful, the concept 
will enable wholly new missions, offer a significant advantage over previously studied 
approaches, or provide a substantial advancement in mission capabilities for NASA or the 
greater aerospace community. 
b. The concept has the potential to generate enthusiasm for the proposed NASA? Mission and 
to build advocacy within NASA or in the greater aerospace community. 
c. The concept study has the potential for notable scientific, engineering, or creative benefits 
that will contribute to its specific field, even if the concept is shown to be infeasible. 
d. There are wider benefits of the study. For example, but not limited to, engaging the public, 
contributing to the national economy, or producing potential non-aerospace spinoffs. 
 
Although all sub-elements will be considered during review, numerical scores will only be 
assigned to the four main evaluation criteria. Each criterion will be scored on a 5-point scale:  
• 5: Excellent; presenting a compelling case with outstanding potential impact, credibility, 
and features .  
• 4: Strong; proposal presents a compelling case with notable potential impact, credibility, 
and features.  
• 3: Good; proposal presents a case with expected potential impact, credibility, and 
features. Note that in NIAC, “3, Good” is the baseline score for a concept that is 
selectable against the criteria.  
• 2: Fair; proposal presents a case with minimally acceptable potential impact, credibility, 
and features.  
• 1: Poor; proposal does not meet minimum satisfaction criteria.

The reviewer scores will be substantiated with text describing the strengths and weaknesses, as

Strength: Positive aspects that make the proposal or denotes superior satisfaction of at 
least one evaluation criterion. Identify any aspects that distinguish the proposal as “top 
tier,” or its satisfaction of at least one evaluation criterion as truly exceptional. Note any 
major strength for a criterion as a comment reserved for observations that significantly 
stand out, and which overrides corresponding weaknesses in that criterion.  
• Weakness: Negative aspects that make the proposal or denotes questionable or inferior 
satisfaction of at least one evaluation criterion. Especially identify any aspects that 
distinguish the proposal as “poor,” or its satisfaction of at least one evaluation criterion 
as notably poor or unacceptable. Note any major weakness for a criterion as a comment 
reserved for observations that significantly stand out, and which overrides corresponding 
strengths in that criterion.  
In addition to scoring each criterion and providing strengths and weaknesses, the technical 
review panel will use the criteria scores to inform a color rating for each proposal as follows:  
• BLUE: Exceptional Quality. Well-conceived and technically sound concept pertinent to 
the goals of the program and the solicitation’s objectives.  
• GREEN: High Quality. Proposal meets the expectations of this solicitation and provides 
a strong opportunity.  
• YELLOW: Acceptable Quality. Proposal marginally meets the expectations of this 
solicitation.  
• RED: Low Quality. Not Selectable; Proposal does not meet the minimum expectations 
of this solicitation; proposal has one or more major weakness.  
• BLACK: Out of Scope. Proposal does not meet the scope or eligibility requirements of 
this solicitation.  
Review Process. The review process is conducted in accordance with the STMD 
Organizational Conflict of Interest (OCI) Mitigation Plan and includes the following primary 
steps. 
 
Technical Panel Peer Review. A technical review panel evaluates proposals against the 
evaluation criteria delineated in this NIAC Appendix. 
 
Prioritized Recommendation and Selection. Responsible NASA officials integrate and 
review the results of the Technical Panel Peer Review for prioritization and presentation to the 
NASA STMD Selection Official. The STMD Selection Official will consider the results of the 
technical evaluation as well as programmatic considerations, such as portfolio balance, 
requested resources, availability of funds, and other programmatic considerations to select a 
portfolio of proposals that is deemed most competitive, represents the strongest investment for 
STMD, and can be supported by NASA within available resources.
