1. RFR Introduction and General Description
1.1 Description
In recent years, the Commonwealth has been experiencing more frequent and intense drought.
Since 2016, nine of the eleven years have had multiple months of drought. These droughts have
caused impacts to water supply, habitat and the natural environment, and are exacerbating fire
danger across the state. With climate change, increasing temperatures and predictions of more
consecutive number of dry days, droughts are expected to increase in Massachusetts. The
Massachusetts Executive Office of Energy and Environmental Affairs (EEA) is soliciting proposals
from municipalities, public water suppliers, nonprofit organizations, public
schools/colleges/universities, and regional planning agencies (RPAs) to participate in a Drought
Resiliency and Water Efficiency (DRWE) Grant Program. The DRWE Grant Program aims to
provide funding for projects that will help the Commonwealth become more climate resilient,
specifically by reducing indoor and outdoor water use, improving water loss control, and increasing
drought management/planning in communities and public water systems across the state. This is a
REIMBURSEMENT program. Target awards are up to $50,000 per project. The program helps
implement actions in the 2023 ResilientMass Plan.¹ For the third year of this program, EEA has
again adopted a relatively broad scope to encourage a diverse range of innovative proposals and is
particularly interested in water efficiency and demand management projects that are not currently
funded through other programs. The focus is on initiatives that promise the greatest long-term
impact on water use reduction, ensuring sustainable water management for the future.
Examples of project types that are eligible include (but are not limited to):
-
Water Loss Control Program Development and Implementation
Comprehensive water loss control program development
Implementation of water loss control audit recommendations including distribution
system leak detection activities and equipment meter calibration services/upgrades
Piloting or demonstrating new and innovative approaches (e.g. regional collaboration,
new technology for leakage reduction and asset management)
-
Water Efficiency Program Development and Implementation
Comprehensive water conservation program/plan development and review of existing
programs
Residential water use (rgpcd) and unaccounted-for water (UAW) compliance plan
assistance
Data management and analysis assistance to inform water efficiency programs (e.g.
compiling and analyzing use sectors and prioritizing largest users)
Residential leak detection activities (e.g. device incentive programs)
Public sector facility/municipal building upgrades to high-efficiency appliances/fixtures
-
Outdoor Water Use Efficiency Improvements
Drought resilient landscape design and/or implementation (i.e., conversions of irrigated
turf to non-irrigated native plant landscapes, etc.)
Irrigation system audits (for functional turf areas)
Irrigation system upgrades to increase efficiency (for functional turf areas)
Drought resilient native plant demonstration/teaching gardens
-
Drought Management Planning
Bathymetric surveys, stage storage curve development, reservoir water level
measurement implementation
Local drought plan development
Development of water use restriction programs

1.2 Background
With the increasing frequency of droughts in Massachusetts due to climate change, the need for

water users across the Commonwealth to find ways to reduce water consumption has become
more urgent than ever. Water efficiency and water loss control are critical tools in this effort. By
reducing the demand for water, communities can better preserve their water supplies during periods
of drought and for fire protection, ensuring both the environment and the population have access to
adequate water resources.
Water efficiency measures such as upgrading infrastructure, installing efficient fixtures, and
encouraging behavioral changes can significantly reduce the strain on water systems, particularly
during peak demand periods. Outdoor water use, which often spikes in the summer months, can be
a major contributor to water shortages, making conservation efforts in landscaping and irrigation
systems vital to long-term water sustainability.
At the same time, addressing water loss through implementing or improving comprehensive water
loss control programs helps ensure that valuable water resources are not wasted. Many public
water systems experience significant losses due to aging infrastructure, leaks, and inefficient
operations. Tackling these losses not only helps conserve water but also improves the overall
efficiency and resilience of the water supply network.
The DRWE Grant Program seeks to fund innovative projects that promote climate resilience by
helping communities and public water suppliers reduce both indoor and outdoor water use, improve
water loss control programs, and increase drought management/planning. By supporting these
efforts, the program aims to create lasting impacts on water conservation and drought
preparedness, helping Massachusetts communities better adapt to the evolving climate and
ensuring the sustainability of their water resources for future generations.
1.3 Eligible Entities
Eligible Entities include Municipalities, Public Water Suppliers, Nonprofit Organizations, Public
Schools/Colleges/Universities, and Regional Planning Agencies. Partnerships between two or more
entities are encouraged.
We recognize that many water resource challenges, including drought management and water
conservation, can often be addressed at a regional scale, where water systems and usage patterns
often transcend municipal boundaries. Regional collaboration allows for a more holistic approach to
water efficiency, as communities and organizations can pool resources, share best practices, and
implement solutions that address broader watershed and regional water supply concerns. With this
understanding, we are encouraging project proposals that incorporate regional-scale
initiatives involving collaborations among multiple organization types, such as
municipalities, public water suppliers, and nonprofit organizations.

3. Evaluation Criteria
Responses to this RFR will be used to rank Applicants. As stated above, responses will not create any
obligation by EEA to the responding parties.
3.1 Evaluation Components
An EEA selection committee will review applications from responding organizations. Applications
will be evaluated based, at a minimum, upon the following criteria:
Alignment with Program Goals (15%)
Proposals will be assessed on how well they align with the program's overall objectives of reducing
indoor and outdoor water use, improving water loss control, and drought planning.
Project Impact (40%)
Proposals must outline expected water savings, improvements in water loss control, and/or
increased drought resiliency, with preference given to those that can deliver significant and
measurable impacts. Proposals should outline clear, measurable outcomes and describe how
project success will be monitored and evaluated.
Additional consideration/preference will be given to proposals that:
incorporate regional-scale initiatives and demonstrate collaboration between multiple
organizations (i.e., municipalities, water suppliers, and non-profit organizations)
demonstrate a long-term ability and commitment to drought resiliency and water
conservation, beyond the immediate scope of the grant period
include innovative approaches or incorporate sustainable practices with long-term benefits
serve as a demonstration project and are transferable to other communities (i.e., innovative
projects that provide deliverables that can be easily adopted by other communities or outline
processes that will streamline other similar projects)
Cost, Budget, Feasibility and Readiness (30%)
Proposals should provide justification for the project's costs relative to its expected outcomes,
including detailed budgets and quotes, as applicable.
