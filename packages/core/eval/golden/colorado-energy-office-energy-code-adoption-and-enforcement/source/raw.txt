Terms Defined
Energy Code Board - The Board authorized to develop the model electric ready
and solar ready code and the model low energy and carbon code pursuant to
House Bill 22-1362, Colorado Revised Statutes 24-38.5-401.
Equivalent or More Restrictive Code - A code that provides equivalent or
greater energy savings, equivalent or greater greenhouse gas emissions
reductions, or stronger prescriptive requirements relative to the 2021
International Energy Conservation Code, the model electric ready and solar
ready code, or the low energy and carbon code.
International Energy Conservation Code (IECC) - A model energy code
developed by the International Code Council that regulates the energy
efficiency and energy use of new residential and commercial buildings.
Local Government - A statutory or home rule municipality, county, city &
county, or special district in the State of Colorado.
Model Electric Ready and Solar Ready Code - A building energy code
developed by the Energy Code Board that applies to new buildings and major
renovations or additions to existing buildings and includes pre-wiring
requirements for future rooftop solar, high-efficiency electric appliances, and
electric vehicle (EV) charging infrastructure.
Model Low Energy and Carbon Code - A building developed by the Energy
Code Board that minimizes overall carbon dioxide emissions associated with
new and renovated homes and commercial buildings. The code is based on the
2024 International Energy Conservation Code, with amendments specific to
Colorado. Cities and counties with building codes must adopt this code, or an
equivalent or more restrictive code code, when adopting or updating any
building code after July 1, 2026.
Partnership - Two or more local governments that jointly apply for funding
through the program.
Subrecipient - A non-federal entity that receives a subaward from a
pass-through entity (in this case, CEO) to carry out part of a federal program.

Section I - Program Overview
House Bill 22-1362 Building Greenhouse Gas Emissions directs the Colorado Energy
Office (CEO) to provide financial assistance in the form of grants to local governments
to assist them in adopting an energy code that meets or exceeds the stringency of the
state minimum energy code. Prior to July 1, 2026, the state minimum energy code
was the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code (IECC) and the Model Electric
Ready and Solar Ready Code developed by the Energy Code Board. As of July 1, 2026,
the state minimum energy code is the Model Low Energy and Carbon Code developed
by the Energy Code Board. HB22-1362 also directs CEO to establish multiple programs
to support local governments in adopting and enforcing the new minimum energy
codes, and training local governments and the building industry to understand and
comply with the requirements of the new codes.
The program will continue to award funds until all funds are expended. The following
program RFA criteria may be modified or waived by CEO unless otherwise required by
law. Any modifications will be posted on the program website; please monitor our
website for the most current version before submitting an application.
The Colorado Energy Office provides a wide range of building code services and
resources, including technical assistance, training, and an adoption toolkit. Visit our
building energy codes webpage for more information.
In the event of conflict between information provided in this RFA and that found
in the terms and conditions discussed in Section VII, the terms and conditions
shall prevail.

2. Allowable Projects & Activities
Proposals must demonstrate the intent of the local government to vote on and/or
adopt as well as enforce, at a minimum, the energy code established by the State of
Colorado, according to the time frame below.
Applications submitted after January 1, 2026 proposing adoption of an energy
code must pertain to the Model Low Energy and Carbon Code, or an
equivalent or more restrictive code. The Model Electric Ready and Solar
Ready Code is incorporated into the Model Low Energy and Carbon Code.
Applications proposing enforcement only of a previously-adopted energy code
submitted after January 1, 2026 must pertain to the 2021 IECC, 2024 IECC,
Model Electric Ready and Solar Ready Code, or Model Low Energy and Carbon
Code so long as the proposed code to be enforced was adopted in accordance
with state law.
Applicants are encouraged to limit the full proposed project timeline to one year of
the start of the award. Applications that propose longer timeframes will be
considered based on the justification for such a timeline provided by the applicant.
Local governments that already meet the state's minimum energy code at the time of
application may apply for energy code enforcement and compliance assistance only,
without voting on and/or adopting additional code updates. However, any applicant
proposing to use funding to adopt a code that is equivalent or more restrictive than
the state's minimum energy code shall commit to voting on and/or adopting the
proposed code.
In addition, it is the responsibility of the local government to demonstrate they will
not weaken the minimum codes with amendments developed during the grant
project. Some options are to include this as a cost in proposals or to seek assistance
from CEO's free technical assistance services.

Partnerships
Applicants who partner with other local governments to collaborate on energy code
adoption, implementation, and enforcement will also be prioritized in this grant

regional collaboration and consistency in code adoption and implementation, more
efficient and effective use of funding, streamlined engagement with technical

should submit a single application, with a single local government, council of

responsible for any distribution of funding among members of the partnership,
fulfilling reporting requirements, and management of all reporting, engagement,
and communication with the Colorado Energy Office on behalf of the partnership.

recognizes that the deployment of building codes cannot be successful without a
robust and diverse building workforce.
Section III - Grant Amounts
Grants awarded through this program will be provided as a reimbursement of
any incurred expenses approved as part of an awarded project. For projects that
involve code adoption, there are two options for payment of these costs in alignment
with Section 11.2 Allowable Projects and Activities:
1. Full reimbursement after the codes have been adopted and/or voted on by the
local governing board, or
2. Incremental reimbursement after tangible milestones towards adoption have
occurred, with 15% of the proposed funding withheld until after the codes have
been adopted and/or voted on by the local governing board.
Applicants proposing the second reimbursement option should include these tangible
milestones with timelines in their project proposal and budget. Should the applicant
fail to achieve any of the proposed milestones, no further payments shall be made to

2. Indirect Cost Rate
Indirect costs are those that have been incurred for common or joint objectives and
cannot be readily identified with a particular final cost objective or grant account. If
the Applicant maintains an indirect rate and chooses to include this on the application
budget, it is expected that the amount budgeted will reflect the approved rate. The
Applicant's indirect costs must be allowable, and comply with the requirements of the
Terms and Conditions of the Federal Awards and 2 CFR Part 200 as amended by 2 CFR
910.
Be prepared to submit one of the following if awarded:
a. Federally Negotiated Indirect Rate Agreement - An entity that receives funding
directly from the federal government must recover indirect costs by using a
federally negotiated indirect cost rate from their Federal cognizant agency.
a. de minimis indirect cost rate - An entity may elect to charge a de minimis rate
of 10% of modified total direct costs as defined at 2 CFR 200.414 if they do not
have a Federally Negotiated Indirect Rate Agreement.
Regardless of the option chosen, CEO requires all indirect rates to comply with
generally accepted accounting principles and be fully supported by actual cost data.

Section V - Application Scoring and Award Process
All applications received under this RFA will be reviewed by a scoring committee that
will consider the following criteria:
1. Demonstrated intent of the local government(s) to vote on and/or adopt and
enforce an energy code that, at a minimum, meets the State of Colorado's
requirements, if a code that meets such requirements has not already been
adopted.
2. Need for funding to achieve the desired energy code update or to implement
an updated energy code, whether or not the applicant meets prioritized
criteria outlined in Section II.3, and any awards already received through this
program.
3. Applicant's technical qualifications to carry out the project, or applicant's
engagement with a technical consultant or other subject matter expert to
assist with carrying out the project.
4. Demonstrated capability, or demonstrated intent to develop capability, to
achieve full enforcement of and compliance with the adopted energy code(s).
5. Energy and/or emissions reduction potential of the applicant's proposed
project.
Applicants will be notified by email whether their application has been selected for
funding or denied. At any point during the selection process, if CEO determines that it
is in the best interest of the State of Colorado, it reserves the right to request
additional information to assist in the review process. Moreover, it reserves the right
to approve selected portions of applications, to reject all applications or to resolicit
the entire grant solicitation. The State may conduct discussions with applicants for
the purpose of promoting understanding of the State's requirements and the
applicant's application, to clarify requirements and adjust services to be performed,
and to adjust prices. Changes to applications, if permitted, will be requested by the
State in writing from applicants. Additional changes may be negotiated during the
development of a grant agreement (see below).
Applicants who believe that they should have been awarded a grant when they were
not or who believe that they were otherwise not properly awarded a grant may
submit their appeal to the Energy Code Program Manager, Adam Berry
(adam.berry@state.co.us). who shall review the appeal and make a final
determination. The sole remedy for Applicants shall be potential funding depending
on availability of funding. Appeals are to be presented within 30 days of the day of
issuance of a grant award decision.

exceptions are to be noted. Applicants agreeing to abide by the requirements of the

increased risk to the State and increased costs. Therefore, the scope of requested
exceptions is considered in the scoring of applications.

Section VII - Awardee Reporting and Auditing
Reporting
Each awardee will be required to:
Provide monthly written reports on the programmatic and financial status of
the project. Reports should include progress on each task in the grant
agreement, a comparison of expenditures and allocations for budget line items,
verification of allowable costs, any difficulties faced during the reporting
period, and an overview of next action items. CEO staff may require additional
items to be included throughout the period of performance.
Meet with CEO staff regularly (up to monthly) to discuss the status of the
project, as CEO determines necessary.
Notify CEO upon adoption of an updated energy code and provide a copy of all
amendments and ordinances.
Report on any local governing meetings where proposed energy codes were
considered but not adopted.
Report on any trainings or certifications provided to builders, contractors,
designers, architects, building officials, or building department staff that will
improve understanding of the adopted energy code requirements.
Provide additional federal reporting such as energy savings, job creation
outcomes, community event metrics, and products developed as part of the
grant (e.g. technical assistance, webinars, training curriculum).
Provide a final report consisting of the programmatic and financial conclusion
of the project, as well as the accomplishment of objectives laid out in the
grant agreement.
Attend a meeting with other awardees to discuss progress of funding support,
share learnings and best practices, and any changes needed to the program.
(This may occur during or after the period of performance)
The lead applicant of an awarded partnership will be responsible for complying with
the reporting requirements listed above. Reporting is required regardless of whether
the subrecipient has requested reimbursements in a given month.
