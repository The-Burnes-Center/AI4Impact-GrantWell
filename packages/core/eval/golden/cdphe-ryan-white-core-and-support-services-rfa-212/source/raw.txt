I.
PROJECT BACKGROUND AND OVERVIEW
In regard to HIV care for people living with HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus), the mission
of the Office of STI/HIV/VH at CDPHE is to provide high-quality services, innovative public
health practice, and community partnerships to improve health outcomes. We envision a
Colorado where all persons impacted by HIV achieve health justice and optimal outcomes by
continuously improving patient care, health outcomes, and satisfaction.
This project is supported by the Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA) of the
U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) as part of an award totaling
$13,219,903 with 48% financed with non-governmental sources. The contents are those of
the Office of STI/HIV/VH at CDPHE and do not necessarily represent the official views of, nor
an endorsement by, HRSA, HHS, or the U.S. Government
The purpose of this CDPHE Request for Applications (RFA) is to solicit applications from
clinics, medical providers, community-based organizations, and other agencies to provide
core and support services for people living with HIV. Due to health coverage options in
Colorado, it is expected that most medical and behavioral health services will be directly
funded by alternative third-party payers; therefore, the majority of funding from this RFA is
expected to support services that "wrap around" clinical and behavioral services in order to
promote or improve access and adherence to HIV care, leading to HIV viral suppression.
To direct resources as needed to support access to HIV-related Core and Support services and
provide local access points for clients who reside outside the Denver metropolitan area, this
RFA solicits responses for the following regional service areas described below:
The Boulder region's designated service area includes the following counties:
Boulder, Broomfield, Clear Creek, and Gilpin Counties. On-site services must be
available in Boulder.
The Northern region's designated service area includes the following counties:
Larimer, Logan, Morgan, Phillips, Sedgwick, Washington, Weld, Yuma. On-site
services must be available in the following priority cities/towns: Fort Collins
and/or Greeley.

Indirect (F&A) Cost Rate:
Definition: Indirect costs are those that have been incurred for common or joint
objectives and cannot be readily identified with a particular final cost objective or
grant account. Indirect costs may be called Facilities and Administration costs (F&A)
at some agencies. Indirect costs are different from administrative costs in most cases.
For example, a program employee can be considered administrative and not be
included in an organization's indirect costs. Costs classified as indirect can differ
depending upon your organizational structure and accounting practices, but some
common examples include depreciation on buildings and equipment, operating and
maintenance costs of facilities, and general administrative expenses such as the
salaries and expenses of executive officers and accounting or legal staff.
If Applicant's organization or business maintains an indirect or F&A rate and
Applicant chooses to include this cost on the application budget, it is expected that
the amount budgeted will reflect the organization's or business' approved rate. Be
prepared to submit one of the following if awarded:
Federally Negotiated Indirect Rate Agreement: An entity that receives
funding directly from the federal government is eligible to recover
indirect costs by using a federally negotiated indirect cost rate from
their Federal cognizant agency. If the entity has a current federally
negotiated rate, CDPHE must honor this rate. HRSA does limit indirect
costs to 10% for this grant, however.

CDPHE Negotiated Indirect Rate Agreement: An entity that does not
have a current negotiated indirect cost rate with a federal agency AND
does not receive federal funds directly from a federal agency can
negotiate an indirect cost rate with the internal audit unit at CDPHE.
Alternatives to a negotiated indirect cost rate:
De minimis indirect cost rate: The de minimis rate of 15% of
modified total direct costs (MTDC) is available to all non-Federal
entities (2 CFR 200.414(f)), though legislation limits maximum

Direct Charge All Expenses: Organizations may elect to direct charge

Private, not-for-profit organizations and public or governmental agencies are eligible to
apply. A for-profit entity is eligible to apply for these funds but will only be funded if a
not-for-profit organization is unable or unwilling to provide quality HIV related service(s).
Not-for-profit applicants are prohibited from serving as a conduit of these funds to a
for-profit entity unless the above is true and verified.
If this application involves the delivery of services that are potentially eligible for Medicaid
funding, the applicant must be certified by Colorado Medicaid as a provider of the
service(s). Proof of such certification must be provided with the application.
No expenditures will be incurred with funds from this RFA for any item or service that may
reasonably be expected to be paid through other state, federal or private benefits programs.
These funds must be used as dollars of last resort and appropriate documentation must be
included in the client file that supports this requirement.

Required Project Components
The CDPHE Office of STI/HIV/Viral Hepatitis is requesting applications from clinics, medical
providers, community organizations, and other agencies to provide services for people living
with HIV. For further information on these services, see the CDPHE HIV Care and Treatment
Standards of Care, which are located here: CDPHE HIV Care and Treatment Standards of

20% of the requested funds for one or more of the Support services.
Applications from non-clinical providers will not be held to the 80% core services
requirement.
Applications from providers that receive Ryan White Part A, C, D or HOPWA must
demonstrate how the funds requested will wrap around and not duplicate other
services.
Applicants requesting MAI funding must have a pre-existing working relationship
and have undergone security clearance with each proposed correctional institution
prior to the submission of the RFA.
Applicants may also request funding for Quality Assurance and Quality
Improvement to provide high-quality services for people living with HIV.

SELECTION, EVALUATION, AND AWARDS
The technical aspects of applications will be assessed based on the soundness of the
applicant's approach and the applicant's understanding of the requirement. Past
experience/qualifications will be assessed by considering the extent to which the
qualifications, experience, and past performance are likely to foster successful, on-time
performance. Technical and past experience assessments may include a judgment
concerning the potential risk of unsuccessful or untimely performance, and the anticipated
amount of State resources necessary to ensure timely, successful performance. The State
may use all information available regarding past performance as defined in
C.R.S. $24-106-107 et.seq.
The program has carefully designed a scoring and selection process to ensure fair selection
of the best qualified applicants. The selection process is described below. The criteria for
scoring are in direct correlation to the required application components.
Applications
that fail to follow all of the requirements may not be considered.
Financial Risk Assessment Rating in Evaluation
The financial risk rating determined from the submitted Financial Risk Assessment
Questionnaire or FRMS rating (Local Public Health Agencies only) will be

communicated to reviewers during the application review process. Applications that
fail to follow all requirements may not be considered.
Consideration of Factor
The evaluation factors, in decreasing order of importance, are:
1. The technical content of the application (25 Points), including
a. soundness of methods proposed to complete the project
b. eligible type of nonprofit organization
C. geographic reach of the proposed project
d. compliance with requirements set forth in the RFA
2. Management capability (45 Points) (qualifications and experience)
a. Applicant's experience, particularly the qualifications and experience of
key personnel assigned to this project.
b. The extent to which the qualifications, experience, and past
performance are likely to foster successful, on-time performance.
C. Applicant's capacity to reach women, infants, children, and youth.
d. Applicant's capacity and processes around billing for services.
3. Budget (30 Points)
a. Is a detailed budget narrative included? Are salaries and details provided
to reflect the number of people assigned to the project?
b. Is the overall budget, as well as the individual items, reasonable
considering the scope of work activities?
C. Is the amount requested reasonable considering the proposed number of
clients to be served?
d. What percentage of the total budget will go to funding client services
versus administrative functions? Applicants that devote a relatively
higher percentage of the budget toward funding client services will be
preferred over Applicants with higher administrative costs. Indirect costs
will be considered an administrative cost.

Application Review Process/Scoring
Applications will be scored using a 100-point scale. The process will be competitive,
based on assigned scores. Applications must achieve 70 points to be considered for
funding.
An Evaluation Committee will evaluate the merits of applications received in
accordance with the evaluation factors stated in this RFA and identify the
application that is most advantageous to the state. This Evaluation Committee
will be composed of CDPHE employees, HIV care professionals, contractors, and
people representing communities affected by HIV. While numeric evaluations
may be used in some aspects of the process to identify strengths and
weaknesses of applications, and to establish a ranking, the final decision will
be a business decision by the State and will not be based on a numerical score.
A Decision Memorandum will document the basis for the award decision.
The technical aspects of applications will be assessed based on the soundness
of the applicant's approach and the applicant's understanding of the
requirements. Past experience/qualifications will be assessed by considering
the extent to which the qualifications, experience, and past performance are
likely to foster successful, on-time performance.
The award will be made to that applicant(s) whose application, conforming to
the RFA, has been determined to be responsive and responsible and most
advantageous to the State of Colorado, considering the evaluation factors set
forth in this announcement. A contract must be completed and signed by all
parties. In the event the parties are unable to enter into a contract in a
reasonable timeframe, the State may elect to rescind the "Notice of Intent to
Make an Award" communication and make the award to the next most
responsive and responsible applicant. The successful applicant(s) is required to
enter into a formal Contract with CDPHE. The State of Colorado reserves the
right to amend the contract in response to future legislative changes that
affect this project.

through Google Forms.
A financial risk rating will be assigned for all applicants. This form must
be completed and submitted by all applicants, including nonprofits,
for-profit businesses, or governmental agencies (review exceptions
below). Applicants must retain a copy of their completed Financial Risk
Assessment Questionnaire for any additional funding applications within
a 12-month period, the same budget period. Any changes will require a
new form to be submitted. The application may not be reviewed if the
completed form is not included. The Financial Risk Assessment rating
does NOT determine whether or not CDPHE will fund an applicant;
rather, it determines an applicant's financial and management strength,
and the level of technical assistance and contract monitoring necessary
to help the applicant succeed with the project if awarded. In order to
incorporate all relevant information, programs must include the
financial risk rating in their initial discussions about which applications
should be provided to the review committees AND during the review
committee evaluations for funding decisions. The final application score
may be impacted by risk determinations made by CDPHE based on
information contained in the form. Scores will be communicated to the
grant review committee and may be considered in the overall score.
Technically, the score could be impacted when the review committee
members see the financial risk assessment score. The form and guidance
are part of this announcement. For more information, please see
Attachment G: Financial Risk Assessment FAQs.
